## The honest thing about this week

It will feel like a waste of time for about two days. You will want to add something to
Project 3.

Do not. **A person who can design and cannot explain gets rejected in round one, and their
document is never opened.** The gap between what you can do and what you can convey is,
right now, the largest single thing between you and the job — and it is by far the cheapest
to close.

---

## What is being assessed

Not whether you know an architecture. The interviewer knows you can look one up.

They are answering one question: **can this person be left alone with an ambiguous
problem?** Which is why a confident wrong answer scores worse than an uncertain right one,
and why "I don't know — here is how I'd find out" is a strong answer.

---

## The two passes that decide it

**Break** and **evolve**. They are at the end of the interview, so they are what gets cut
when the clock runs out — and they are where the hiring decision is made.

Everything on Tuesday is about protecting them: announce the budget in the first minute,
timebox pass 1 out loud, and say "I'll come back to that" and mean it.

## A note on sources

This week's three articles are the only ones in the course with **no primary sources at
all**, and they say so at the top.

Interview advice is overwhelmingly Tier 3 and 4 — folklore repeated until it sounded true.
Ours is an argument from what a design conversation is for, and you should treat it exactly
as sceptically as week 1 asked you to treat everything else. If a piece of it does not
survive contact with a real interview, discard it.
