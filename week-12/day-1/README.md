# Day 1 — The story of a design

> **By the end of today** you can talk about each of your three projects for ninety
> seconds, and each version contains reasons rather than components.

## The exercise

Three projects, three lengths, out loud and recorded.

**Ninety seconds each.** The problem, the interesting constraint, and what you decided. No
components.

Do each one **three times**. The first will be a list of boxes. By the third the components
have fallen out and the reasons have arrived, and that transition is the whole exercise — it
does not happen by thinking about it.

Then the five-minute version of your favourite: the same, plus the two decisions you are
proudest of and **one you got wrong**.

---

## The five questions

Have an answer ready for each, about each project:

1. What would you do differently?
2. What was the hardest part? *(a decision, not a technology)*
3. What broke, or what would break?
4. Why not the obvious alternative?
5. How did you know the numbers? *(given, estimated, or invented — say which)*

Question 5 separates the strong answers. "I assumed 5x peak because I could not measure it,
and at 20x the queue tier triples" beats any real number, because it shows the shape of
your uncertainty.
